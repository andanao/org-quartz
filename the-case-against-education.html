The Case Against Education

  • economics
  • nonfiction
  • book

Feb 24, 2025

This book presents a fun and odd moral connondrum for me, and also really drives home how far into the minority I am with my own interests and things that I like doing, the people that want to take college/graduate coursework in their spare time and like reading academic papers for the kick of it are in the minority of people by far (though this minority does seem to benefit from the way that the world is currently structured). Much like The Dictators Handbook its interesting to read something that you agree with but that criticizes and presents a mroe logical reason against something that you strongly believe in and support thinking it does good, and trying to find ways to be able to mesh the new things that you’ve learned into your worldview.

Caplan’s basic point is that education is about 80% Signaling and 20% Human Capital, that is 80% of the value of education is signalling to employers that you are the sort of person that is worth hiring, and only 20% of education is teaching skills that are useful in the job market. I think few people will disagree with the idea that education is largely signalling, why tf did I study so much bs in HS like history that really has nothing to do with anyone’s job, and no one really remembers jack shit once they graduate.

His suggestions are clear and radical:

  • Cut education budgets, the money can be better spent in far more places, people are too educated to be doing the things that they are doing in the workforce. This includes **scholarships and funding, and suggests raising tuition
  • All students should do their best to finish HS (signalling, but the workforce significantly punishes you for not finishing) and they have to be there anyways
  • Push more students towards apprenticeships and vocational degrees, learning something is better than nothing
  • Get rid of child labor laws and allow teens (his limit but who knows) to work, students who are failing or who love work and hate school aren’t doing anyone any good by staying in school and wasting their and societies time and resources
  • Open up schooling curricula to allow for more exploration and more relevant topics, almost one needs history, art, literature, music, soical studies, geography, etc. to do their jobs, and even the ‘more useful’ topics such as science and higher math are used by very few of the students who take them. Time should be allowed to let students play and figure things out on their own (he says that students learn more when playing than when being taught, but I’m not sure what play is being defined as)
  • If the purpose of school is to make pupils into dilligent, conformist and hard working adults make schools more like military academies rather than what they are now

Everyone now in the US is far too educated for the jobs they have, and few people ever need the degree they studied for in college to do their jobs, the simple requirement of a college degree in order to do one’s job for something that doesn’t require it is simply vetting potential applicants since companies don’t have the ability to give everyone a shot, a degree ensures that applicants are capable of working hard enough, smart enough, and are conformist and diligent enough to do what they are told. Beyond basic fundamental skills that everyone needs to survive in the modern economy most schooling is a waste.

An interesting perspective that he has was that we decry child labour as abusive and exploitative, and robbing children of their childhood, but when we force children to sit in classrooms and listen to teachers drone on about shit they barely even care about and teach to the test, we tell the kids to ‘lump it’ and ‘that’s the way it is’ but we used to say the same about child so ? idk just an interesting perspective that I’ve never heard before

The idea that school ‘expands’ students minds and opens them up to a broader world is kinda BS as well, like how many students end up falling in love with the high culture that gets forced down their throats by the educational system, and personally I think that there are far more things that there is to be learned from things that people find interesting than what the educational system thinks is valuable.

Thoughts on The Case Against Education :thoughts:book:

I really agree with basically everything that he states, and his final suggestions are logical given what he says. The problem is I love school, learning, and high culture, but that really puts me in the minority worldwide, few people want to learn all the time every day and do it in their free time and want more free time to keep learning. And even that doesn’t mesh with the fact that so much of what was learned is useless to the world that we all live in, and pushing everyone to go to college without thinking of what the implications of what they study entails is ruinous and when there are no job opportunites for them, the number of yearly phych grads almost outnumbers the total number of working phsychologists, which means that people who genuinely want a career in that must pursue further education rather than weeding the people out that don’t need it.

So taking this book and things that I’ve discussed with other people in mind I think:

  • More technical vocational skills and more variety of them. Want to learn to code and just do that for work? Go for it! Standard office skills, sure! Use your hands in what may be a dying industry (go full send its not like an anthro degree pays)
  • More realistic looks at potential job outcomes for going to university and what that entials, if you’re a below average student what makes you think that you’ll do well in a more tough and strenuous college environment? Maybe working is for you
  • Freedom within the curricula, I think people will learn and do more with what they are interested in and not too many people want to spend their lives learnign things all the time, but there are things that interest people more than others, I think having a bigger plate to chose from and making them take a few veggies but not a ton is the way to go, K-12 education for the most part is a lot of babysitting anyways
  • This ties into the vocational skills, but even if something is basically useless such as PE or music, there is a lot to be said for the discipline that these things can instill and the long term development of a skill over time can apply to far more things once people get older and learn more stuff, so I think treating them as a total write off isn’t a good idea only if people stick with it to the point that they develop genuine mastery/love for what they are doing.
  • In terms of the cost of higher education, I think that poor people should be encouraged and allowed to attend college, it shouldn’t be price discriminatory, however higher and more equitable admission standards then pricing based on a % of the potential amount that can be paid and the need that is evaluated is the way to go. I’m ok (well kinda) with the idea of loans, but loan forgiveness needs to be an option if for example the degree is something that is deemed ‘critial’ like engineering or whatever, but only for people that end up finding jobs in the relevant ‘critical’ fields.
  • I love the liberal arts, and I think they shoud continue to be funded and get more funding, but the teaching of these topics to people that are just there for a peice of paper is a waste of everyone’s time, so many students don’t care what the prof is saying and the prof would rather be doing their research that pays that students in my opinion who don’t know what they are goind to be doing in college need to have a serious talk about what the potential options are and weigh their outlooks on things. I’m cool with trying to cull the heard in the number of students who are getting liberal arts degrees, so long as the social sciences are getting funding to compensate for the lost number of students, more research wil be better, and it’s not like there’s a shortage of qualifed applicants.
  • Going to college should not be the default option and when people are thinking of going, there should be a serious conversation about what the potential outcomes are for each thing and what the reasons for going are, ‘becase it’s what you do’ should not be an acceptable option, but also people who are genuinely super interested and just want to learn because its what they love should be encouraged. I haven’t thought about this enough, but maybe teachers along with a personality test could be able to determine how genuine that seems, and demonstrating previous interest in what they would like to pursue (or general area, too many specific choices for one thing)
  • The job of K-12 education should be to genuinely expand students minds, but not towards the fixed things that teachers have been taught, instead I think that kids should be allowed tons of freedom so long as the things that they are spending their time on could be considered edifying in some way, and all people have intersts and while it shouldn’t be 100% play, education should be more towards learnign how these interests work and such.

I think the ideal day would have 1-2 hours of: here’s the shit that you have to learn or that we think you might find interesting, then the rest of it is the constrained free time where people are free to learn about the things that they are intersted in.

  • I’d rather not open up child labor laws, but rather encourage more technical skills that could be applied to work, maybe it might be some work skills, if the students like it they could do work, but I’m not sure how I feel about doing paid work at the age of 12, but I do think that it can’t come at the cost of more school work, I think it is important to realize that there does need to be time to figure shit out and live life as a kid to some extent (god the 高考 is the last thing I want).

Damn this book has really stuck in my head and I’m glad I read it, but fuck is there a lot to think about given how much I love and support education

Thoughts while reading

Damn I hope I don’t become a liberatarian, cause this and The Dictator’s Handbook and some other stuff that I’ve read really make me support some stuff, but I do really believe that the role of government is super importatnt because the free market is not a good solution when we want equitable results not efficient results.

Its fun to read things that you really disagree with and that challenge my worldview

It is absoluetley fascinating to hear his criticisms of online schooling in the COVID enviornement when everyone is online. It really drives home the idea that everyone is there for the god damn paper for the signal it sends.
